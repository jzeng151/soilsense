# Web localization

## Supported languages

The web app and planner support English (`en`), Spanish (`es`), Korean (`ko`),
and Simplified Chinese (`zh-Hans`).
`src/shared/language.ts` is the shared web/planner language inventory, including
BCP 47 locales for `Intl` formatting. The Twilio phone line intentionally remains
English/Spanish only; web language support does not claim Korean or Chinese telephone speech.

The language selection is saved only in this browser as `soilsense.language`.
`LanguageProvider` updates `<html lang>` and applies the preference across the
planner, navigation, evidence, Sprout, and supporting pages. Sprout receives an
explicit, server-validated response-language instruction and forwards the language
to its planner tool. Product names and source citations stay unchanged.

## Why Chinese is the follow-up language

Verified live from the U.S. Census Bureau on 2026-11-15: **Chinese (including
Mandarin and Cantonese) is the largest named non-English home-language category
after Spanish** in the 2025 ACS 1-Year Estimates, table B16001, United States,
population age 5 and over. Estimates are Spanish **44,921,985** (90% margin of
error ±177,787), Chinese **3,775,645** (±47,062), and Tagalog (including Filipino)
**1,969,835** (±39,681).[2][3][4]

The ranking was recomputed across all 42 published non-English language/category
estimate rows, not inferred from UI locale popularity or the number of people
with limited English proficiency. The ACS groups some languages and does not
measure preferred writing script; survey estimates have sampling uncertainty.[2][3][4]
The [recorded ranking](i18n/census-language-ranking.json) includes variables,
90% margins of error, retrieval time and the public Census table-data/metadata
URLs. The generic Census API redirected unauthenticated queries to a missing-key
page; the data above was retrieved through the same public JSON endpoints used
by the live data.census.gov table, with no credentials or third-party dataset.

**Script choice (product decision, not a Census finding):** use Simplified Chinese
(`zh-Hans`, visible label 简体中文; `Intl` locale `zh-CN`) as one consistent initial
written-Chinese translation. The Census Bureau itself publishes separate Simplified
and Traditional Chinese language guides, so treating one script as universal would
be inappropriate.[5] This release does not provide `zh-Hant` or claim that Simplified meets
every reader's preference. Add Traditional as a separate reviewed translation,
not an automatic claim of coverage for all Chinese varieties. Written Chinese
support does not add Mandarin/Cantonese telephone speech.

Chinese copy is also AI-translated and has not yet been reviewed by a native
speaker. Chinese mode displays:

> 简体中文内容由 AI 翻译，尚未经中文母语者审校。

Chinese agricultural terms include 大豆, 玉米, 冬小麦, 谷物高粱, 覆盖作物,
灌溉 and 轮作. Numbers, US units, product names, verbatim source quotations and
all scientific caveats follow the same preservation rules as Korean.

## Translation disclosure and scientific scope

Non-English copy may be machine/AI-translated. A translation is not evidence of
review by a native speaker, farmer, agronomist, or extension specialist. Translation
review and agronomic validation are separate tasks. The Korean text introduced
here is AI-translated and **has not yet been reviewed by a native Korean speaker**.
Korean mode displays this note visibly on every page:

> 한국어는 AI가 번역했으며 아직 한국어 원어민의 검토를 받지 않았습니다.

Keep original measurements, numbers, comparison periods, units, source names,
research quotations, and uncertainty caveats. Formatting a date or number with
`ko-KR` does not convert inches, acres, pounds, bushels, USD, or yield units.
Verbatim third-party research and unknown provider text remain in their original
language rather than receiving unsafe fragment substitutions.

Agricultural vocabulary includes 대두 (soybean), 옥수수 (corn), 수수 (grain
sorghum), 겨울밀 (winter wheat), 피복작물 (cover crop), 관개 (irrigation),
and 윤작 (crop rotation). NASA POWER, MODIS, SMAP, IMERG, GRACE and USDA
remain product/agency names, not translated descriptions.

## Adding copy safely during parallel development

English is the canonical fallback. New-language dictionaries are partial records:
a concurrently added English key must not fail TypeScript or render `undefined`.
`translate` uses the requested translation when present, otherwise the exact
English copy (including the long safety/tradeoff overrides). The How-it-works
paired-copy helper follows the same fallback policy. Page-specific dictionaries
also fall back to English.

`web/src/Korean.i18n.test.ts` reports missing Korean keys without failing on
coverage alone; it does fail if a supplied translation corrupts placeholders.
Page and How-it-works dictionaries have separate coverage checks. Re-run the
coverage report after merging new English/Spanish features and translate omissions.
Unknown user/provider text is not a dictionary key and is preserved.

## Verification

Run `npm run typecheck`, `npm test`, and `npm run build`. The language tests
exercise request validation, distinct plan cache keys, deterministic summaries,
glossary entries, agricultural names, interpolation, and English fallback. Existing
English/Spanish deterministic outputs and golden engine snapshots must remain
unchanged.

Run the built Worker locally with the offline configuration, then run the
accessibility/keyboard runners with `A11Y_BASE_URL` pointing at that local port.
Inspect Korean at 375 px after fonts finish loading; verify `<html lang="ko">`,
the disclosure, line wrapping, disclosure-expanded evidence, and the absence of
horizontal page overflow. `word-break: keep-all` and Korean system-font fallbacks
improve readability; long identifiers and URLs must still wrap or scroll within
their own panels. Automated axe results are not WCAG certification or a substitute
for native-speaker and screen-reader review.
